2.1

Governance — Atana BIM Policy

Everything on this page operates under Atana's Project Information Management Policy (SA-ATANA-I-PST-CEO-0004). The policy is binding on all applicable projects and personnel and establishes the Common Data Environment (CDE) as the single source of truth for project information, aligned with the ISO 19650 series, ISO 9001 and ISO 27001.

Policy Commitments

  1. Mandatory use of the approved CDE as the single source of truth for all project information.
  2. Information management (IM) common processes applied consistently, aligned with ISO 19650.
  3. Information exchanges are controlled, traceable and auditable — supporting ISO 9001 and ISO 27001.
  4. Digitally traceable exchanges in accordance with the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, 2002.

Electronic Information Exchange

Information exchanged through the approved CDE is recognised as a legally valid data message. Approvals and authorisations captured in the CDE constitute valid electronic signatures, supported by system controls and audit trails.

No email attachmentsInformation is shared as controlled links to the CDE, never as email attachments — ensuring only the latest approved version is accessed by permitted individuals.

Five BIM Principles

5.1

Information as a Strategic Asset

Managed with appropriate control, accountability and quality throughout the lifecycle.

5.2

Clarity of Information Requirement

Level of Information Need is clearly defined and agreed, aligned to the digital plan of works.

5.3

Consistency and Standardisation

Standard processes, naming conventions and metadata ensure consistent, repeatable delivery.

5.4

Collaboration and Transparency

Shared information, defined responsibilities and structured workflows, all visible within the CDE.

5.5

Open and Structured Data

Delivered using OpenBIM and recognised industry standards for interoperability and long-term value.

Scope of Application

Proposal Delivery Close-out Contractually required BIM Internal digital / quality / risk objectives

Applies to all Atana personnel, project teams and appointed parties, regardless of contractual BIM requirements. Where client-specific requirements conflict, contractual obligations take precedence.

2.2

Digital Asset Delivery Framework

This framework sits alongside the BIM Policy as foundational governance: it shows how organisational, asset and project information requirements relate to one another, and how they converge into the Information Models that are actually delivered. Outlined to ISO 19650-1.

Requirements

OIROrganisation Information Requirements — information needed at an organisational level, e.g. to support asset management.
AIRAsset Information Requirements — sets out the OIR for project-specific contractors or in-house teams, for a specific asset.
PIRProject Information Requirements — information needed to answer the Appointing Party's strategic objectives for a built asset project.
EIRExchange Information Requirements — the information to be delivered by project teams.

Deliverables

AIMAsset Information Model — single source of digital information related to an asset, supporting the organisation's asset management system.
PIMProject Information Model — single source of digital information developed during the design and construction phase of a project.

OIR encapsulates into the AIR and contributes to the PIR. The AIR specifies the AIM; the PIR contributes to the EIR, which specifies the PIM; the PIM in turn contributes to the AIM.

3.1

Project Lifecycle & Governance Documents (ISO 19650-2)

Every Atana project moves through the eight information management stages defined in ISO 19650-2, clause 5. Each stage has defined documents and a named responsible role. Stages 1.6–1.8 are where Atana's Forma Information Exchange Process applies in operational detail — see the linked steps.

1.1

Assessment and Need ISO 19650-2 §5.1

The Appointing Party defines its organisational, project and asset information requirements, and consolidates them into the Exchange Information Requirements (EIR) — alongside the project's information standard, protocol and CDE strategy.

OIR — Organisational PIR — Project AIR — Asset EIR — Exchange Information Requirements
Led by: Appointing Party · RACI detail →
1.2

Invitation to Tender ISO 19650-2 §5.2

The EIR is issued to prospective tenderers as part of the invitation to tender package, together with reference information, response requirements, delivery milestones and evaluation criteria.

EIR issued Reference information Evaluation criteria
Led by: Appointing Party · RACI detail →
1.3

Tender Response ISO 19650-2 §5.3

Tenderers respond with a pre-appointment BIM Execution Plan, Mobilisation Plan and Risk Register, demonstrating their proposed approach, capability and capacity to meet the EIR.

Pre-appointment BEP Mobilisation Plan Risk Register
Led by: Appointed Party · RACI detail →
1.4

Appointment ISO 19650-2 §5.4

Proposals are confirmed and made contractually binding: the BIM Execution Plan, detailed Responsibility Matrix and Master Information Delivery Plan are finalised, and sub-appointed parties develop their Task Information Delivery Plans.

BEP Responsibility Matrix MIDP TIDP(s) Sub-appointed EIR
Led by: Appointed / Sub-Appointed Parties · RACI detail →
1.5

Mobilisation ISO 19650-2 §5.5

Resources, information technology and production methods are mobilised and tested — including the CDE, shared resources and information exchange between task teams — ahead of collaborative production.

CDE configured & tested Production methods tested Team resourced & trained
Led by: Appointed / Sub-Appointed Parties · RACI detail →
1.6

Collaborative Production of Information ISO 19650-2 §5.6

Task teams generate, coordinate and quality-check information within the CDE, then review and approve it for sharing. This is where Atana's Forma Information Exchange Process (Steps 1–6) applies in operational detail.

Generate & coordinate QA check Approve for sharing
Led by: Sub-Appointed / Task Teams · RACI detail → · Forma Steps 1–6 →
1.7

Information Model Delivery ISO 19650-2 §5.7

Information is submitted for Lead Appointed Party authorisation, then submitted to the Appointing Party for review and formal acceptance as a deliverable. Atana's Forma process (Steps 7–10) governs this in operational detail.

Submit for authorisation Review & authorise Review & accept
Led by: Lead Appointed Party / Appointing Party · RACI detail → · Forma Steps 7–10 →

The project's Common Data Environment is archived and lessons learned are captured for future projects. Aligned with Forma Step 11 — Asset Acceptance.

Archive CDE Lessons learned

3.3

CDE Status Codes & Revisions

How ISO 19650 status codes and revision numbers change as a deliverable moves through the CDE. This builds directly on the States Overview above — read this if you need to know exactly what code and revision to expect at each point.

Key Principle A document's Status Code and Revision must always reflect its current state in the CDE. S-codes and D-codes belong to the Shared state and are non-contractual. A-codes, B-codes and CR belong to the Published state and represent authorised, contractual information. When a document moves from Shared to Published, both the Status Code and the Revision are updated.

When a document is approved, it's automatically copied from Shared into Published. The Shared version stays exactly where it is and keeps its Shared-state status — but is flagged to show it has been published, so staff always know which Shared revision produced which Published one.

Golden Rule

  • S and D codes are for information under review in the Shared state.
  • A, B and CR codes are for information formally authorised and Published.
  • When information moves from Shared to Published, the Status Code and Revision must both be updated to reflect its new contractual status.

3.4

Forma Information Exchange Process

This 11-step process is Atana's operational implementation of ISO 19650-2 Lifecycle Stages 1.6 (Collaborative Production), 1.7 (Information Model Delivery) and 1.8 (Project Close Out), executed within the Forma Common Data Environment. Click any box in the diagram to jump straight to that step. Governance Review, Delivery Validation and Client Review are approval gates — where information does not pass, it returns to WIP for correction before re-entering the process.

4.1

Information Production & Coordination (WIP)

WIPSuitability S0States overview →

Project information is created and developed within the Work In Progress (WIP) environment using approved authoring and collaboration platforms. Design coordination, clash detection and issue management are undertaken in Revizto before formal issue. A typical WIP status is S0 — under development, not for client review or contractual reliance.

Process Summary

  1. Author information within Revit, Civil 3D, Teams or SharePoint.
  2. Develop and refine the design within the WIP environment.
  3. Coordinate models and information in Revizto.
  4. Identify and resolve coordination issues, clashes and review comments.
  5. Complete internal discipline reviews and quality checks.
  6. Confirm deliverable readiness for formal issue.
  7. Submit the deliverable for verification and entry into Step 2.

WIP Applications

Autodesk Revit Autodesk Civil 3D Microsoft Teams Microsoft SharePoint Revizto Forma WIP

Coordination Activities

Federated model reviews Clash detection Design review meetings Issue identification Conflict resolution

4.2

Information Verification

WIPSuitability S0States overview →

The TTIM verifies deliverable readiness, coordination requirements and information quality. The TTM performs the discipline approval and confirms the deliverable is suitable for formal issue — the final quality check before formal issue, performed within the WIP environment.

Process Summary

  1. TTIM reviews the deliverable for completeness and compliance.
  2. Outstanding comments, issues or corrections are addressed by the project team.
  3. Coordination issues and review comments are confirmed as resolved.
  4. TTM performs the final discipline review.
  5. TTM approves the deliverable for formal issue.
  6. Deliverable is ready for upload into the Forma workflow process.

Responsibilities

Both paths converge before the workflow is initiated at Step 4 (3a → 4 in the process summary above).

4.4

OCR Metadata Capture

WIPSuitability S0States overview →

The TTM executes the OCR process to populate key metadata fields, including the work stage, document title and revision. This metadata forms the basis for workflow validation and information retrieval, and is completed before the Information Manager review stage.

Process Summary

  1. Start the workflow in Forma.
  2. Execute the OCR process on the uploaded PDF.
  3. Extract the Work Stage, Document Title and Revision from the title block.
  4. Review the extracted metadata for accuracy.
  5. Correct any discrepancies where required.
  6. Submit the deliverable for Information Manager review.

4.9

Client Review

SHAREDClient SharedStates overview →

Where applicable, the client reviews the submitted information and provides comments or acceptance. Information requiring revision is communicated to the PDM, who directs the Task Team and returns the deliverable to the WIP state to re-enter the approval process.

Process Summary

  1. Client receives the deliverable through the formal information exchange process.
  2. Client reviews the information and provides feedback.
  3. Comments and actions are recorded and assigned.
  4. Project team addresses comments where required.
  5. Revised information is resubmitted if necessary.
  6. Accepted deliverables proceed to publication approval.

Review Outcomes

OutcomeAction

Communication Loop — Rejected / Revision Required

Where the client rejects a deliverable or requires revision, the outcome is not actioned directly by the client — it is communicated back through a single point of contact:

Client→ PDM→ Task Team→ Step 1 — WIP

The PDM notifies the Task Team of the outcome and directs correction; the deliverable then re-enters the process from WIP and works back through Steps 1–8.

7.2

Metadata & Standards — Classification

Atana uses Uniclass 2015 to structure digital assets across the project and asset lifecycle. It is embedded directly in the naming convention and exists throughout the CDE (Forma) — every model, drawing, document and Revit family carries a classification code.

In the Naming Convention

Two Uniclass-derived attributes are embedded in every information container name:

FiForm — the type of information container (e.g. model, drawing, schedule), drawn from Uniclass's Form table.
RoRole — the discipline or authoring role, derived from Uniclass's Information and Project Management tables.

Because these are captured at naming-convention level, classification is enforced automatically the moment a container is created in Forma — not added retroactively.

Digital Assets & Revit Families

Three Uniclass tables are essential to structuring digital assets and Revit families, enabling downstream digital twin use:

SsSystems — how components group into functional systems (e.g. heating, drainage).
EfElements/Functions — the functional part of a building an object belongs to.
PrProducts — the specific product or object type itself.

Where Classification Is Used

Ss — Digital Plan of Work breakdown Ss / Ef / Pr — Clash detection grouping in Revizto Ss / Ef / Pr — Digital twin structuring Fi / Ro — Naming convention & Forma metadata

BIM Uses Enabled by Classification

Digital Plan of Work Structuring

The DPoW is broken down using the Ss (Systems) table, aligning deliverables to consistent, project-wide system groupings rather than ad-hoc discipline lists.

Clash Detection & Coordination

Ss/Ef/Pr classification lets Revizto filter and group clash results by system, element or product — turning raw geometry clashes into coordination issues a discipline can act on. See FORMA Step 1.

Digital Twin Enablement

Consistent Ss/Ef/Pr coding on Revit families means asset data carries structured, machine-readable meaning from design through to the AIM — the foundation a digital twin depends on.

Search, Retrieval & Reporting

Fi/Ro naming attributes and Ss/Ef/Pr object classification make information findable and filterable across Forma and Power BI, without relying on folder structure alone.

Content Consistency

The Content Catalog enforces the same Uniclass codes at the point of family/parameter creation, so classification is consistent before content is ever used on a project.
